# Rent Roll Lite — The Free Product

**Build this first.** Before the paid product, before the sequences, before any automation.

## Why it comes first

The unknown is not whether we can build a good spreadsheet. It is whether a commercial property
manager will trade an email address for one. That question costs a weekend to answer with Lite
and three weeks to answer with the full Control Center.

It is also the only mechanism in the plan that produces subscribers, and subscribers are what the
revenue arithmetic actually requires — 1,200 to 4,300 of them
(`.claude/skills/building-distribution/SKILL.md` §2).

---

## What it contains

| | |
|---|---|
| Lease rows | **10** |
| Expiry chart | One, three years forward |
| Action list | One, sorted by notice deadline, RED/YELLOW/GREEN |
| Format | Excel + Google Sheets |
| Price | Free, for an email address |

## The limit, chosen deliberately

**Ten rows.** Obvious, and not crippling.

A property manager with a 40-lease portfolio can put their ten most urgent leases in and get a
real answer this week. That is genuinely useful — useful enough to keep, and to mention to
someone. Then the eleventh lease is a wall they hit while the tool is already working, which is
the only moment a $199 upgrade is an easy decision.

**What Lite must not be:** a demo, a teaser, a locked file, or a spreadsheet with the formulas
removed. If it is not useful on its own it will not be kept, and a free product nobody keeps
produces no subscribers and no referrals.

**What Lite must not contain:** the exposure analysis, the tenant-concentration view, the renewal
pipeline, or the five-year calendar. Those are the paid product. The line is *urgency* (free) vs.
*analysis* (paid) — not quantity of the same thing.

## The test it exists to run

Per `revenue-engine` §8, Phase 1 is **100 tightly qualified prospects** and it is a listening
exercise, not a measurement. What we are listening for:

- Do they recognise the problem in the first line of the email?
- Does "rent roll" land better than "lease rollover"? (The keyword data says yes. This tests it
  against actual humans.)
- Do they download?
- Do they come back with a question, and what is it about?
- Does anyone ask for more than ten rows?

**Do not compute a conversion rate from 100 prospects.** Report counts and quotes. A rate needs
n ≥ 100 in the *denominator that matters*, and at this stage that denominator is downloads, not
sends.
