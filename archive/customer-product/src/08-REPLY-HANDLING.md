# Reply Handling

Ten kinds of reply. What each one means and what to do about it.

**The general rule:** reply to anything human the same day. Interest decays fast, and a reply is
the scarcest thing your outreach produces.

---

## 1. Positive — they're interested
*"Yes, we're looking at that." · "What would it cost?" · "Can you come and see it?"*

**Do:** reply the same day. Don't send a price. Ask for a short call or go straight to proposing a
walkthrough.

> *"Glad to. I'd rather see the space before putting numbers to it — fifteen minutes on site and
> I can give you a real figure. Would Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon suit?"*

**Don't** quote over email. You'll have to pad it, and they'll compare your padded number to
someone else's padded number.

---

## 2. Neutral — they want information
*"What's this about?" · "Send me some details."*

**Do:** answer briefly and specifically, then ask one question back. A generic brochure kills this.

> *"We do recurring commercial cleaning around [area] — nightly and weekly. Before I send anything,
> what would be most useful to know: frequency, what's included, or rough pricing?"*

---

## 3. Objection
*"Too expensive." · "We're happy with ours." · "Not a fit."*

**Do:** acknowledge, ask one clarifying question, then respond to the actual answer. Don't argue
and don't discount.

Most objections at this stage are a request for a reason to feel safe. See **10 Proposal System**
for the fuller list.

---

## 4. Wrong person
*"That's not me — try Dana."*

**Do:** thank them, ask for the introduction, update your records.

> *"Appreciate it. Would you mind pointing me to the right person, or passing this along?"*

**Worth more than it looks.** A named internal referral is a warm route into a building.

---

## 5. Not now
*"Circle back in the new year."*

**Do:** pin down a month, then leave them alone until it.

> *"No problem. Roughly when would make sense — after the new year, or nearer spring?"*

**A "not now" without a date sits in your list forever.** Get the date. Then actually wait.

---

## 6. They already have a provider
*"We've got someone, thanks."*

**This is not a loss.** It's the normal state of a commercial building, and timing is the whole
point of this method.

**Do:** ask one question and go.

> *"Good — that's how it should be. When does that arrangement come up for review? I'll check back
> then rather than bothering you before."*

**The renewal date is the single most valuable thing a "no" can give you.** Put it in your tracker,
set a reminder for sixty days before, and you have a scheduled future conversation.

---

## 7. Unsubscribe
*"Remove me." · "Stop emailing."*

**Do:** suppress immediately and permanently. No reply. No "sorry to hear that."

One opt-out list, covering email and phone. Never re-add, whatever happens later.

---

## 8. Hostile
Rare. Occasionally someone is rude.

**Do:** suppress. Don't reply. Don't defend yourself. Nothing good follows.

---

## 9. Out of office
**Do:** pause and resume after the date they gave. Don't count it as a reply.

---

## 10. Bounce
**Hard bounce** — the address is dead. Mark it invalid and stop. Repeated hard bounces damage your
ability to reach anyone.
**Soft bounce** — full mailbox or a temporary problem. Retry once, then stop.

---

## Two numbers to watch

**How fast you answer positive replies.** Same business day, ideally within a couple of hours. This
is the highest-return habit in the whole system and costs nothing.

**What proportion of replies are class 6** — "we already have someone." If it's most of them, your
outreach is working and your *timing* is off. Chase triggers harder rather than writing new copy.

---

## What not to do

**Don't auto-reply to anyone human.** Someone who replies to a cold email is rare. An instant
automated answer wastes it, and people can tell.

**Don't count "we're covered, thanks" as a positive reply.** It's polite, and it's a not-now. If
you count it as positive, your numbers will look fine while nothing moves.

**Don't send a sixth email.** If five touches produced nothing, stop. It won't win the contract
and it costs you the chance to come back when something changes.
