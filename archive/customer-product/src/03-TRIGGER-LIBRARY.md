# Trigger Library

Sixteen public signals that a commercial building might be worth contacting now, ranked by what
they actually tell you.

## The rule that governs all of them

**A trigger is an event, not a conclusion.**

You saw a permit filed. You did not see their cleaning contract.

State the event, say what you do, and offer. Never state what you think the event means. Buyers can
tell — everyone guessing at them sounds identical, and someone who states a checkable fact sounds
like a person who did the work.

**Every trigger needs a source and a date**, or it scores zero regardless of type. Unsourced
triggers are how invented claims get into emails.

---

## The four tiers

The tier tells you *what kind of thing you know*, which matters more than how exciting it sounds.

| Tier | What it means | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Buying event** | They have said, in public, that they are buying | **5** |
| **2 · Facility event** | A building changed in a way that creates a cleaning need | **4** |
| **3 · Organizational event** | The organization changed in a way that may put vendors up for review | **3** |
| **4 · Secondary signal** | Activity. Not evidence of a cleaning need | **1** |

**Modifiers:** +1 a second independent trigger · +1 if under 30 days old · −2 past its window ·
**0 if source or date is missing.** Cap at 5.

**Tier 1 breaks ties.** A boosted facility event can reach 5, but a stated solicitation is a
different kind of knowledge, and when two accounts tie you work the Tier 1.

# Tier 1 · Buying events

**The only triggers where the building has told you it is buying.** Everything else on this page is
inference, however good.

## 1. Janitorial contract out to bid
A school district, municipality, county, hospital or housing authority publishes a solicitation
for cleaning services.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | State, county, city and school-district procurement portals. Most publish openly; many send email alerts. Search "janitorial", "custodial", "building services". |
| **Fresh for** | Until the deadline — check it first, it is often short |
| **Record as** | *RFP 2026-114, custodial services, Northside ISD, posted 12 Aug, closes 6 Sept* |

**Why almost nobody uses it:** it looks like paperwork. It is the strongest signal available.

### Verify before you spend an hour on it

Public bids reward preparation and punish improvisation. Check these in order and stop at the
first one that rules you out:

1. **Is it current?** Portals keep expired notices visible. Confirm the closing date.
2. **When does it close?** Many give two to four weeks. If it closes Friday and you have not
   started, it is not for you.
3. **Can you bid at all?** Vendor registration, bonding, insurance minimums, business licensing,
   and sometimes a minimum number of years trading or comparable contracts. Some require
   certifications you cannot get in time.
4. **Prevailing wage?** Many public contracts require it. It changes your labour cost completely,
   and a bid priced at your normal rates will lose money if you win it.
5. **What is the actual scope?** Square footage, frequency, specialty work, day porter,
   supplies. Read the whole document — the scope is often larger than the title suggests.
6. **Is there a mandatory walkthrough?** Frequently yes, and missing it disqualifies you.
7. **Is the incumbent bidding?** Usually. Public records often show the current contract value —
   which tells you the price to beat, and whether it is worth beating.

**The honest warning:** public cleaning contracts are often price-driven and lower margin than
private work, and the paperwork is real. A fair number are not worth winning. Verify first, bid
second, and be willing to walk away at step 4.

## 2. RFP for related facility services
Grounds, maintenance or facility services going out to bid at a site.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | The same portals |
| **Fresh for** | Until the deadline |
| **Why it counts** | A site reviewing one vendor category often reviews others. Weaker than a cleaning RFP, stronger than any inference. |

# Tier 2 · Facility events

**A building changed in a way that creates a cleaning need.** Strong, but you are inferring the
need — they have not said it.

## 3. New facility or first occupancy
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Occupancy and tenant-improvement permits on your county or city portal |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |
| **Record as** | *TI permit #2026-04412 finaled 2 Aug, City of Plano* |

**No incumbent to displace.** A first purchase, not a switch.

## 4. New location opened
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | A new Google Business listing under an existing brand · "now open" notices · local press |
| **Fresh for** | 120 days |

## 5. Move or relocation
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Address change on their Google listing · "we've moved" notices · business-journal columns |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |
| **Why it lands** | They are buying everything at once and want it off the list |

## 6. New commercial lease signed
A listing flips from available to leased.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Commercial property listing sites. Check the same handful weekly and note what changed. |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |
| **Why it is useful** | Often 30–60 days ahead of the occupancy permit, so you arrive early |

## 7. Suite expansion
They took the unit next door.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Listing changes · updated square footage · a second suite number appearing |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |
| **Why it is easy to win** | Their current cleaner is priced for the old footprint, and someone has to reprice it |

## 8. Competitor cleaning company closing or sold
Their accounts need a vendor.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | A Google listing going "permanently closed" · a business-for-sale listing · local talk |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |

**Handle this carefully.** Approach the *buildings*, not the competitor's staff or client list, and
never mention the competitor by name. This industry is small and it will get back to people.

## 9. Post-construction completion
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | The permit portal, filtered to finaled commercial permits |
| **Fresh for** | 60 days |
| **Note** | Post-construction cleaning is a separate, usually larger, one-off job. Price it separately. |

# Tier 3 · Organizational events

**Something changed at the organization.** Vendors may come up for review. May.

## 10. Property management change
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Portfolio pages on management company sites · listing sites · signage |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |
| **Why it is worth the most per conversation** | New managers review inherited vendors, and one relationship can cover several buildings |

## 11. Building sold or changed owner
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | County property records, searchable in most counties |
| **Fresh for** | 120 days |
| **Why** | New owners review operating costs, and cleaning is an easy line to look at |

## 12. Facilities or operations hiring
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Indeed and LinkedIn — Facilities Manager, Office Manager, Operations Manager |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |

**Two-edged.** They may review vendors, or be too new to change anything for six months.

## 13. Renovation or reopening
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Renovation permits · reopening announcements · temporary closure on their listing |
| **Fresh for** | 60 days |

## 14. Acquisition, new business licence, or sign permit
Three routes to the same conclusion: something new is operating at an address.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Business journals and registries · city business-licence records · sign permits on the same portal as occupancy |
| **Fresh for** | 90–120 days |
| **Note** | A sign permit is often the earliest public signal a new tenant exists, and it is cheap to check |

## 15. New provider or partner joining a practice
| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | Staff pages on medical and dental sites |
| **Fresh for** | 90 days |
| **Why** | More chairs, more rooms in use, more cleaning |

# Tier 4 · Secondary signals

Activity, not evidence of a cleaning need. Use to break a tie between accounts that already have a
Tier 1–3 trigger.

## 16. Headcount growth · funding · reopening · seasonal · review mentions

A company that raised money has more money. It does not have a dirtier office.

**Seasonal** is the exception worth planning around: schools and childcare before term and before
the summer break. Not an event, but a real window, and the only one you can diary a year ahead.

### Review mentions cleaning — why this is Tier 4 and not Tier 1

It feels like the strongest signal in the library. It isn't.

A review saying the restrooms were filthy does not tell you the business controls the cleaning
contract, that the reviewer was an employee rather than a customer, that the problem is current, or
that anyone is looking. It is an inference about dissatisfaction, not an observation of a buying
window.

**Never quote a review to a prospect.** It reads as surveillance, and it asserts something about
their vendor you cannot support.

## What you may and may not say

| You observed | You may say | You may not say |
|---|---|---|
| A published cleaning RFP | "Saw the custodial solicitation — are you taking bids from new vendors?" | Anything about the incumbent |
| Occupancy permit | "Saw the new building on Commerce cleared occupancy" | "You don't have a cleaner yet" |
| New Google listing | "Saw you opened the second location" | "You'll need a cleaner there" |
| Address change | "Noticed you moved to Commerce Street" | "Your old cleaner won't follow you" |
| Building sold | "Saw the building changed hands in July" | "The new owners will want to cut costs" |
| Portfolio addition | "Saw you picked up the Elm Street building" | "The inherited vendor won't work out" |
| Facilities job post | "Saw you're hiring a facilities manager" | "They'll want to replace their vendors" |
| Competitor closed | "I understand you may be looking for a new cleaning provider" | The competitor's name, or why they closed |
| Review complaint | *nothing* | *anything* |

Everything in the right column is a conclusion.

## Checking weekly — about 40 minutes

| Check | Where | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Procurement portals** | State, county, school district | **Weekly, and set up email alerts** |
| Permits — occupancy, TI, sign, renovation | County or city portal | Weekly |
| Listing changes on your accounts | Google Business | Weekly |
| Facilities and ops job posts | Indeed, LinkedIn | Weekly |
| Commercial lease listings | CRE sites | Weekly |
| Management company portfolios | Their websites | Fortnightly |
| Property sales | County records | Fortnightly |
| Business journal | Local press | Fortnightly |

**Set the procurement alerts first.** It is the only source on this list that emails you when a
building decides to buy.

**Freshness is the whole advantage.** A 120-day-old permit is history, and mentioning it reads as
careless. Work the newest first.
