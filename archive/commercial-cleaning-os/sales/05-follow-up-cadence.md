# 05 — Follow-Up Cadence

**Instance B.** Applies from proposal onward. Earlier-stage outbound sequences live in
`../outbound/sequences/`.

**Principle:** professional, short, and finite. Never imply a reply is owed. Never send the same
message twice with a different date.

**Every follow-up carries a reason to exist** — new information, a genuine deadline, or a
straightforward close-out. "Just checking in" is not a reason, and buyers read it as pressure
without content.

---

## 1. After a proposal

| Day | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Email | Proposal + "I'll call Thursday" |
| 2 | **Phone** | The walkthrough call. Where questions actually surface |
| 5 | Email | One useful addition — a reference, a scope clarification, a photo from the walk |
| 10 | Phone | Direct: *"Where's this sitting?"* |
| 17 | Email | The close-out |
| — | — | **Stop.** → `Nurture`, review in 90 days |

**Five touches over 17 days, then stop.** A sixth does not win the contract; it costs you the
chance to come back later.

### The close-out email — the highest-converting message in the sequence

```
Subject: Closing the file on MacArthur

Hi Dana — I'll assume the timing isn't right and close this out so I stop
cluttering your inbox.

The proposal stands if anything changes. If you'd like me to check back
when the current arrangement comes up for renewal, just tell me roughly
when that is.

Either way, good luck with the new space.
```

It works because it is genuinely final, it asks for one small piece of information instead of a
decision, and it removes pressure. A meaningful share of replies to a well-run cadence arrive here.

---

## 2. After a walkthrough with no proposal decision

Rare — usually means you did not get the proposal out in 48 hours. Fix the cause, not the cadence.

---

## 3. Nurture

The long game, and where trigger-based outbound compounds. Most commercial buildings are already
served; the question is always *when*.

| Situation | Cadence |
|---|---|
| Happy with incumbent, renewal date known | One touch **60 days before renewal** |
| Happy, renewal unknown | Every 6 months, light |
| Lost on price | 6 months, then annually |
| Lost to a competitor | **90 days.** New vendors underperform early more often than not |
| Timing wrong | At the date they named |
| In-house | 6 months |

**Nurture touches must carry something.** A relevant note about their building type, a seasonal
reminder (floor care before winter salt), a genuine observation. Three "just checking in" emails
train someone to ignore your name permanently.

**A `nurture_review_date` is mandatory** (`../crm/lifecycle-stages.md` §2). Nurture without a date
is a graveyard with better branding.

---

## 4. Rules

**Stop immediately on:** any opt-out, however phrased · a request to stop contacting ·
a clear final no.

→ Suppression, global, permanent, both channels (`../crm/routing.md` §4).

**Never:** more than 5 touches in one cadence · two channels the same day · a "checking in" with no
content · fake urgency · guilt (*"I haven't heard back…"*) · re-enrolling from `Nurture` without a
review date arriving.

**Every touch is an Activity row.** If it is not logged, it did not happen — and you will send a
sixth.

---

## 5. Cadence health

| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proposal → decision, median days | Sets a realistic forecast |
| % of proposals reaching close-out with no reply | High = the proposal or the qualification is weak |
| Replies to the close-out email | Consistently the highest-response touch |
| Nurture → reactivation rate | Whether nurture is real or theatre |

If most proposals reach day 17 in silence, the problem is upstream — discovery did not qualify, or
the proposal did not answer the question they actually had. **Do not fix it by adding touches.**
