# Personas

**ICP = which companies. Persona = which person at those companies.** Get the persona wrong and a
perfectly targeted account produces nothing.

**The rule from `revenue-engine` §5:** a CRM title does not make someone a decision-maker.
`is_decision_maker = confirmed` requires that they said so, or a published document says so.

---

## Instance A — who we write to at a cleaning company

### P1 — Owner / Founder · **primary, ~70% of the list**
Runs a 3–25 person operation. Often still on job sites. Reads email on a phone between stops.
Signs everything.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Actually worries about** | Where the next contract comes from · crew retention · whether growth is worth the headache |
| **Says** | "commercial accounts", "bidding", "walkthroughs", "recurring", "getting in the door" |
| **Never says** | "pipeline", "ICP", "sequence", "TAM", "outbound motion" |
| **Buys when** | It's concrete, quick, and looks like something they could actually run |
| **Ignores** | Anything that sounds like a marketing agency; long emails; jargon |
| **Reach at** | Personal work address from the site's about/team page, or a GBP review reply signature |

**Writing rule:** no marketing vocabulary, ever. The moment an email reads like an agency wrote it,
they classify it with the twenty others they got this week.

### P2 — Sales / Business Development Manager · secondary, 15–25-staff companies only
Owns commercial growth, does not own the budget. Reports to P1.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Worries about** | Hitting a number · where next month's walkthroughs come from |
| **Buys when** | It makes them look effective. Understands "system" better than P1 does |
| **Blocker** | May still need the owner's sign-off on a purchase |

**Give them something forwardable.** If P2 replies, they are a champion, not a decision-maker —
ask who else is involved before quoting.

### P3 — Operations / General Manager · rare
Reachable, rarely the buyer for growth tooling. Route to P1.

### Who we do not write to
Cleaners and crew leads · office administrators without commercial responsibility · franchise
managers · anyone at a national FM company.

## Instance B — who the customer writes to at a facility

**This is the map that decides whether a walkthrough happens.** Getting it wrong wastes the trigger.

| Segment | Who signs | Who to write to | Do **not** write to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Property management** | Property Manager; Portfolio Manager for multi-site | Property Manager named on the building listing or the firm's team page | The leasing agent |
| **Office / professional** | Office Manager under ~50 staff; Facilities Manager above | Office Manager | The CEO or managing partner |
| **Medical / dental** | **Practice Manager or Office Manager** | Practice Manager | **The physician or dentist** |
| **Industrial** | Operations Manager or Plant Manager | Operations Manager | HR |
| **School / daycare** | Director, Administrator, or Business Manager | Director for small sites; Business Manager for districts | Teachers |
| **Fitness** | General Manager; Regional above | GM | Front desk |
| **Retail** | Store Manager — **only if not chain-decided** | Store Manager | Corporate, unless multi-site is the play |

### The mistake that costs the most
**Pitching the physician when the practice manager holds the vendor relationship and the budget.**
Medical is the highest-value segment and the one where this error is most common. The doctor is not
the buyer; they will forward it, or more likely delete it.

### Persona detail — the three that matter most

**Property Manager.** Manages several buildings for owners they answer to. Judged on tenant
complaints and cost control. *Worries about:* a tenant escalating to the owner · vendor
reliability · being blamed. *Wins with:* responsiveness commitments in writing, and a vendor who
can cover more than one building. *Trigger that lands:* they just took on a new property.

**Practice / Office Manager (medical).** Runs the whole non-clinical operation. Compliance is not
optional and cannot lapse. *Worries about:* an inspection · a patient complaint about a restroom ·
staff doing cleaning they should not be doing. *Wins with:* protocol specificity, documented
frequency, reliability. *Trigger that lands:* new location, or a move.

**Office Manager (professional).** Facilities is one of eight things they own. Wants it handled and
invisible. *Worries about:* being the person who has to chase the cleaners · the office looking bad
when clients visit. *Wins with:* one point of contact and a response time. *Trigger that lands:*
the move itself — they are buying everything at once and want it off the list.

## Contact discovery — cheapest first, stop when you have a name

1. **Website** — about, team, leadership, contact. Highest hit rate for this segment, free.
2. **Google Business Profile review replies** — owners and managers sign them by name. Consistently
   underused and free.
3. **Secretary of State registry** — officers and registered agents. Free, public, reliable for
   small operators.
4. **LinkedIn** — company page → people → filter by role.
5. **Enrichment tool** — last, and as *verification*, not discovery. The known failure mode for
   small local businesses is returning a generic inbox rather than the owner.

### Routing by what you found
| Found | Route |
|---|---|
| Named person + verified email | Full trigger treatment, personalized |
| Named person, pattern-guessed email | Verify first. `unverified:` **may not be used in outreach** |
| Generic `info@` only | Short, plain, **written to be forwarded** |
| Nothing | Drop, or phone if capacity allows. Do not build a second machine for it |

**`info@` is not worthless — it is only worthless when the email assumes it reached the owner.**
Written for the person who forwards it, it earns its place.

Expected named-human coverage: **55–70%**. Anything higher usually means guesses are being recorded
as facts.
