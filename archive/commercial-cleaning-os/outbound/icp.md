# ICP

Two ICPs, one per instance. Confusing them produces campaigns aimed at the wrong companies.

**Rule that governs both:** the ICP is a filter for where to spend effort, not a claim about any
individual account. An account passing the filter still has to be researched, scored and verified
before anyone contacts it.

---

## Instance A — who we sell the OS to

> **A US commercial cleaning or janitorial company with 3–25 staff, an active website with a
> commercial services page, independent ownership, and a findable named owner or sales lead — that
> already services recurring B2B accounts and wants more of them.**

### Inclusion
| Criterion | Test | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial focus | Site has a commercial / office / janitorial / facility services page | Residential-only cleaners have no use for this |
| Size band | 3–25 staff (team page, fleet, review volume, service-area claims) | Below 3, no capacity to service new contracts. Above 25, they likely have a salesperson and buy agencies |
| Independent | Not a franchise of Jan-Pro, Coverall, ServiceMaster, Vanguard, Anago, Stratus, Buildingstars, Corvus, Office Pride | The local owner usually does not control marketing |
| Named human | Owner, founder, president, or sales/BD lead findable by name | Generic inbox only means no route to a decision |
| Alive | Reviews or site activity in the last 12 months | |
| Geography | One of 10–15 target metros | See below |

### Exclusion — score 0, disqualify
Solo residential house cleaners · franchises · national facility-management firms · brand-new
businesses with no operational capacity · companies with no commercial offering · anyone on
Suppression.

### Geography — this is the correction that matters
**DFW is the demonstration market, not the sales territory.** One metro yields a few hundred
qualified cleaning companies; the conservative model needs roughly 3,500–4,000 to reach the target
(`../../research/11-corrections-log.md` §A / Correction 4).

**Sell into 10–15 metros. Build one DFW pack as the public demo. Build every buyer a pack for their
own city on demand.** A DFW-only pack breaks the offer for an Atlanta buyer.

Suggested set, chosen for commercial formation and construction density: Dallas–Fort Worth ·
Houston · Austin · San Antonio · Phoenix · Atlanta · Charlotte · Nashville · Tampa · Orlando ·
Raleigh–Durham · Denver · Las Vegas · Jacksonville · Kansas City.

### Addressable size — measured, not cited
US Census reports ~**67,799 employer establishments** in NAICS 561720 (Janitorial Services). Industry
sources cite 1.2M+ across a broader universe including non-employer entities — **different
definitions, not different measurements**.

**Neither is our addressable market.** Ours is whatever the ICP scrape returns after the filters
above. The DFW scrape-and-qualify pass settles it empirically in an afternoon, and that number is
worth more than any headline count.

## Instance B — who the customer pursues

> **A commercial facility in the customer's service area, large enough to be worth a walkthrough,
> where the cleaning decision is made locally.**

### Segment priority
| # | Segment | Why this rank | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Property management** | One relationship can expose several buildings. Highest leverage per conversation | Longer procurement; portfolio value not first-building value |
| 2 | **Office / professional** | Largest addressable pool, straightforward scope | Most contested |
| 3 | **Medical / healthcare** | Highest rate per sqft; compliance-driven frequency that cannot lapse | Only where the customer is genuinely qualified — waste handling, protocols |
| 4 | **Industrial / light industrial** | Larger contracts | Specialist scope, safety requirements |
| 5 | **Retail / commercial** | Volume | Often has in-house closing staff |

Schools, daycares and fitness sit under 5 as secondary campaigns — stable and less contested, but
lower value and often seasonal.

### Qualification filters, applied in order
| # | Filter | Drop when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Physical commercial premises | Home-based, virtual office, PO box, service-area-only listing |
| 2 | Size floor | Under ~1,500 sqft — the contract cannot justify a walkthrough |
| 3 | Size ceiling / decision locus | National chain or corporate campus where cleaning is contracted at HQ |
| 4 | Active | No reviews or updates in 12 months |
| 5 | Not self-cleaning | Restaurants and retail with in-house closing crews, absent a specialty trigger |
| 6 | Reachable | No website **and** no phone |
| 7 | Serviceable | Outside the customer's area or capability |

**Expected yield is roughly 20–25% of raw scraped places.** Plan list-building around that, not
around the raw count.

### Explicitly not assumed
That every account is a fit · that any company has a current need · that a trigger means they are
shopping · that a public review proves an incumbent is being replaced · that a title makes someone
a decision-maker.

## Instance B is configurable — it is a product feature

The customer's ICP is **theirs**, not ours. The Core OS ships this as a worksheet they complete:
segments they can service, radius, size floor and ceiling, capabilities they have and lack, and
segments to exclude.

A cleaning company that only does offices within 15 miles should end up with a materially different
target list than one doing medical across a metro. **If the product hands both the same list, it is
a directory, not a system.**
