## THE PROBLEM

You can buy a list of every business in your city this afternoon. It won't help.

A list tells you who exists. It doesn't tell you which buildings are worth your time, who signs the
cleaning contract, or whether there's any reason for them to take your call this month. So you
email a thousand offices, they all already have a cleaner, and nothing happens.

**A list is not a pipeline.**

A pipeline is a list plus three things: a reason each account is worth pursuing, a named person who
can say yes, and a reason to make contact now rather than someday. Add those and the same thousand
names become forty worth working.

Most commercial buildings already have a cleaner. They're not switching because your email was
better written. They switch when something changes — they move, they open a second location, a new
building comes under management, someone gets hired whose job is vendors.

Those moments are visible in public records. This system is how you find them.

## THE MECHANISM

```
ACCOUNT → PERSON → TRIGGER → OUTREACH → FOLLOW-UP → WALKTHROUGH
```

**The order matters, and most people run it backwards.**

Start with the **account** — which buildings can you actually service, at a size worth a
walkthrough. Then the **person**, because at a dental office the practice manager signs and the
dentist doesn't. Then the **trigger** — the dated, public reason this account is worth contacting
this quarter.

Only then do you write anything. Outreach built on a real trigger says something specific and
checkable. Outreach built on a list says "we provide high-quality cleaning services," which is what
everyone else says.

Get the order wrong and you personalize an email to the wrong person at a building you can't
service about a reason you made up.
