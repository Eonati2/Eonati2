# Sell anything, honestly

The aim is to make the right buyer feel understood, sure and safe, then make buying easy. Persuasion only works when it is true. A trick that is caught costs trust, and with the FTC's 2024 fake-review rule it can also cost money.

## 1. Before writing: know the buyer (JTBD)

Answer these four questions in one line each before writing a word:

1. **Who** is it for? Example: an engaged couple, 3–9 months out, doing it themselves, on a budget.
2. **What job** are they hiring the product to do? Example: "Make my wedding look designed without paying a stationer."
3. **What fear or pain** do they have? Use their own words, which you can find in Pinterest search suggestions, Reddit r/weddingplanning, reviews and questions. Wedding research (2025) shows:
   - **money** is the top stressor;
   - **pressure for it to look "perfect"** is close behind (about half of couples cite it);
   - **"social-media-worthy"** pressure matters too (54% of Gen Z);
   - **overwhelm and anxiety** affect about a third.
4. **What proof** will remove doubt? For example: what's included, the exact format ("edit in Google Slides, not Canva"), a size list, instant download, "print one proof first".

## 2. The psychology levers (Cialdini + behavioral)

Use at most one or two per asset, and only if true.

| Lever | Honest use for us | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Clarity (the #1 lever)** | Product name, what's in it, price, how it's delivered | Vague "elevate your day" lines |
| **Anchoring** | A real comparison: "$27.99, $4 less than buying both" and "12 signs for $12, about $1 a sign" | Inventing a "was" price the item never sold at |
| **Social proof** | Real sales or review counts, but only once they exist | Fake reviews, invented "1,000+ couples love it" |
| **Scarcity / urgency** | A real seasonal deadline ("print 2 weeks before"), or a real sale with a real end date | Fake countdowns, or leaving an expired offer on the page (we did this; see the log) |
| **Authority** | Specifics show expertise: sizes, fonts, print tips | Claims we can't back |
| **Reciprocity** | Useful free tips in pins (seating chart how-to) | Bait-and-switch |
| **Commitment** | A small first yes: the Stationery Suite at $12, then an upsell to the Collection | Pressure |
| **Loss / pain** | Name the real pain ("guests texting 'where do I go?' all weekend") | Shaming |
| **Decoy / good-better-best** | Kit $19.99 / Suite $12 / Collection $27.99, with the bundle as the obvious value | Fake tiers nobody can buy |

## 3. Copy formulas

- **PAS** (Problem → Agitate → Solve) is best for pins and short descriptions, because buyers already feel the problem.
- **AIDA** is for longer pages and emails.
- **Pin description pattern** (it works for us, so keep it):
  - keyword-first opening sentence that names the problem;
  - what's included, using 2–3 keywords;
  - one true reason to buy;
  - the CTA with the price, for example "Get all 12 signs for $12, instant download."
  - Length: 220–330 characters.
- **Headlines:**
  - say the search phrase plainly, because a clever line that hides the topic loses on Pinterest;
  - include "Wedding" (this is the user's rule);
  - 5–8 words;
  - one idea only.
- **Tone:** sound like a friend who has planned a wedding. Use short sentences and "you" more than "we". No hype words (amazing, ultimate, stunning), no emojis or hashtags in pin copy, and no ALL CAPS shouting.
- **Every claim must be checkable** against the product: the count of signs, sizes, software and price.

## 4. Design that sells (pins, mockups, covers)

- Use a 2:3 vertical format, 1000×1500.
- The text overlay is the biggest lever. Make it high-contrast and bold, readable at thumbnail size, and keep it off busy parts of the image.
- Show the product **large and in use**: a styled scene with flowers, paper texture or a real table setting. Don't use flat previews.
- Keep one focal point, one headline and one CTA pill with the price.
- Add seasonal refreshes, such as winter backgrounds in Nov–Jan and engagement-season hooks in Dec–Feb.
- Brand subtly. The product is the hero, not the logo.

## 5. "Will this work?" check (run before publishing anything)

Act like a picky human buyer, then like a QA tester.

**Five-second test:** at thumbnail size, can you tell *what it is, who it's for and what it costs*? If not, rewrite the headline.

**Truth check:**
- Does every number match the live product (12 signs, $12 / $19.99 / $27.99)?
- Are there any expired dates, discounts or "new" labels older than about 30 days?

**Wording check:**
- Is the keyword in the headline, title, description and alt text?
- Do spelling and grammar hold up?
- Does any text overflow or get cut off?
- Does every link carry the right UTM tag?

**Emotion check:**
- Does it name a real feeling, such as relief, pride or "finally done"?
- Does it avoid fear-mongering?

**Friction check:**
- Is it clear how they get it and what happens after paying?
- Are the objections answered (Canva? printing? editing?)?

**Rules check:**
- No ads.
- No price changes unless the user asked.
- No logo account.
- No fake urgency or reviews.
- Wedding rule followed.

**Breakage check:** what happens if this runs 100 times?
- Duplicates?
- A stale offer?
- Quota or timeout limits?

Fix what you find before posting. If you can't fix something, say so plainly.

## 6. Always learn: the loop

1. **Before** any campaign, read the LESSONS LOG below and the latest weekly review.
2. **Change one variable** at a time: headline, image or CTA. Note it in the manifest.
3. **Measure the right number:**
   - outbound clicks and saves, not just impressions;
   - then Gumroad views → sales.
4. **After** results arrive (the weekly review), add 1–3 dated lines to the log under Worked or Failed. Keep it short and factual.
5. When something wins twice, promote it into the rules above or into `pinterest-organic-playbook`.

## LESSONS LOG (newest first, keep updating)

### Worked
- 2026-11-08: The user's push for 10 pins went out cleanly. What made it work:
  - keyword-first descriptions with a "$12, instant download" CTA;
  - "Wedding" in every image headline;
  - one pin per workbench cell, which avoided the 60s timeout.
- 2026-11-05: The user's WEDDING RULE ("Wedding" in every image headline) makes it clear in the feed who the pin is for. Keep it.
- Bundle anchoring: "$27.99, $4 less than buying both" is true and simple. Keep it.
- Concrete objection-busters on product pages work as reassurance: "Not a Canva template", "edit free in Google Slides", "print one proof first".

### Failed / fix
- 2026-11-10: The expired LAUNCH15 sentence ("15% off until Nov 7") stayed on all 3 Gumroad pages for 3 days after it ended.
  - Rule: every dated offer gets a removal reminder on the day it is created.
  - Rule: the truth check scans for any date that has passed.
- 2026-11-08/09: 14 queued pins still said "9 signs" after the Suite grew to 12. Old creative goes stale when the product changes.
  - When a product changes, re-scan the whole pin bank and Gumroad copy for the old facts.
- 2026-11-09/10: About 15 hourly pin runs were missed because of worker restarts and plan mode. The daily volume goal (12–15 a day) was missed.
  - Keep each run to one self-contained cell.
  - Report missed runs honestly.
- BlogToPin: 37 of its pins were rejected (about 26 credits to remake; still waiting on the user's decision). Look for the rejection reason before spending more credits.
- 0 sales so far (Nov 10). Traffic is the bottleneck: keep volume and test headlines on outbound clicks, and don't change prices.
