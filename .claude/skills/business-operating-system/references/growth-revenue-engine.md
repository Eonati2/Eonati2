# Growth and Revenue Engine

Use this file for new businesses, growth plans, revenue targets, advertising decisions and scaling. It adds four engines to the base organization: Market and Competitor Intelligence, Sales and Conversion Intelligence, the Revenue Model, and Scaling Rules.

## 0. Clarify the target first

When the owner states a money goal (for example "$1,000,000 business"), do not assume what it means. If the distinction changes the plan, ask once:

> "Does $1,000,000 mean (a) annual revenue, (b) cumulative revenue, (c) annual profit, or (d) company valuation? And by when?"

Until answered, show the plan for annual revenue as the default **and say that it is an assumption**. The four targets need very different plans: $1M valuation can come from much smaller revenue, and $1M profit needs much larger revenue.

## 1. Market and Competitor Intelligence (every new business)

Lead: Market Research Analyst. Support: Creative Strategist, Pricing Specialist, Growth Director.

| Step | Output | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Target market | Who buys, where, why now | Define one primary segment first |
| 2. Current demand | Search volume, platform trends, marketplace bestseller signals, seasonality | Use tools (keyword/SEO tools, platform trends, marketplace searches); cite source and date |
| 3. Real competitors | 5–10 named competitors with links | Only real businesses found in research |
| 4. Winning offers | What top competitors sell, format, bundles, guarantees | Describe what is observable |
| 5. Pricing and positioning | Price table, positioning statements | Cite pages |
| 6. Reviews and complaints | Top praise and top complaints from real reviews | Quote short snippets with source |
| 7. Underserved needs | Gaps between complaints and offers | Label as Recommendation |
| 8. Differentiation | 2–3 ways to stand out the owner can deliver | Must fit the owner's idea |
| 9. Acquisition channels | Ranked channels with evidence (where competitors and buyers are) | Rank by evidence, not habit |

**Never present assumptions as verified research.** If a tool is unavailable, say which data is missing and how to get it.

## 2. Sales and Conversion Intelligence

Lead: CRO Specialist / Funnel Architect / Copywriter, depending on the task.

| Area | What to produce |
|---|---|
| Customer psychology | Jobs to be done, emotions, identity, risk felt at purchase |
| Buyer intent | Intent level by channel (search = high, social discovery = lower) and the matching message |
| Pain points | Ranked list in the customer's own words |
| Purchase motivations | Desired outcome, status, time saved, money saved, relief |
| Objections | Price, trust, fit, effort, timing; with an answer for each |
| Direct-response copy | Hook → problem → solution → proof → offer → CTA |
| Offer development | Core product + bonuses + guarantee + clear deliverables; value must exceed price |
| Pricing psychology | Anchors, tiers, charm prices, bundles (Pricing Specialist approves logic, owner approves prices) |
| Landing page | Above the fold: who it is for, outcome, product image, price, CTA; then proof, details, FAQ |
| Checkout | Fewest steps, clear total, trust signals, mobile friendly, payment options |
| Upsell / cross-sell | Relevant next item at the right moment, with margin check |
| Retention | Post-purchase email, usage help, review request, loyalty or repeat offer |

Ethics: persuasion must be truthful. No fake urgency, fake scarcity, fake reviews or hidden fees.

## 3. Revenue Model

Lead: CFO. Support: Pricing Specialist, Growth Director, Attribution Analyst.

### Core formulas

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue | orders × average order value (AOV) |
| Orders | visitors × conversion rate |
| Gross profit | revenue − cost of goods sold (COGS, incl. platform and payment fees, fulfillment) |
| Contribution margin per order | AOV − COGS per order − variable fees |
| Customer acquisition cost (CAC) | marketing spend ÷ new customers |
| Net profit | gross profit − ad spend − operating expenses − taxes |
| LTV (simple) | AOV × purchases per customer over the period × gross margin % |
| LTV:CAC | LTV ÷ CAC (healthy target is commonly cited around 3:1; treat as a guideline, not a law) |
| Break-even CPA | contribution margin per order (before ads) |
| Break-even ROAS | AOV ÷ contribution margin per order |
| Break-even point (units) | fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit |
| Working capital need | inventory + prepaid costs + ad spend float + payment payout delay costs, until cash returns |

### Work backward from the target

1. Target (for example $1,000,000 annual revenue).
2. ÷ AOV = orders needed per year → per month → per day.
3. ÷ conversion rate = visitors needed.
4. Split visitors by channel (organic, paid, email, partners) using real channel data or labeled assumptions.
5. Paid share × CPC (or CPA) = ad budget needed.
6. Check: does contribution margin cover CAC? If not, the plan must first raise AOV, conversion or margin, not spend.
7. Build milestones: first sale → first $1,000 month → break-even month → $10k month → … → target run-rate. Each milestone has the metric that must be true to move to the next.

Always show three scenarios (conservative, base, optimistic) and label every input as Verified (owner data) or Assumption.

## 4. Scaling Rules (advertising)

Never scale because of clicks, impressions or CTR alone.

Scale a campaign only when **all** are true:
1. **Confirmed purchases** in the store/payment system (not only platform-reported), enough to be meaningful (state the count).
2. **CPA below break-even CPA**, with a margin buffer the CFO sets.
3. **Attribution quality** rated medium or high by the Attribution Analyst (platform numbers reconcile reasonably with real orders).
4. **Conversion rate stable** over at least several days, not one lucky day.
5. **Cash flow** can carry the higher spend until revenue arrives (payout delays, inventory).
6. **Owner approval** for the new budget.

How to scale: increase budget in small steps (commonly 20–30% every few days), watch CPA after each step, and roll back if CPA rises above break-even. Duplicate winning ads into new audiences only after the base campaign is profitable.

When to stop or fix:
- High CTR + no sales → the problem is the page, offer or price, not the ad. Send to CRO.
- Low CTR → creative or targeting problem. Send to Creative Strategist.
- Sales but CPA above break-even → raise AOV (bundles/upsells), improve conversion or cut weak segments before spending more.

## 5. Execution capabilities (with approval)

Where the owner authorizes and the tools exist, the organization can build or improve:

| Asset | Lead role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Websites, landing pages | CTO + CRO + Copywriter | Build as drafts or on staging; publish only with approval |
| Online stores, product listings | E-commerce Manager | Draft listings; owner publishes or approves |
| Sales funnels | Funnel Architect | Map first, then build step by step |
| Customer databases / CRM | Customer Service and CRM + CTO | Respect consent and privacy |
| Email automation | Email Specialist | Flows built inactive until approved |
| Reporting dashboards | Data and BI + Attribution Analyst | Read-only data sources |
| Business integrations | CTO | Only through official APIs, MCP servers or approved connectors |
| Advertising workflows | Ad specialists | Campaigns created paused; owner turns them on |

Never claim an asset was built, connected or launched unless a tool action in this conversation did it and the result is shown.
