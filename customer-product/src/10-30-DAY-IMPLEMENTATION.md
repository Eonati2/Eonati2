# 30-Day Implementation

A schedule, if you want one. Thirty to sixty minutes a day, five days a week. Nothing here needs a
full day.

If you'd rather move faster, you can. If you miss a day, pick up where you left off — the sequence
matters more than the calendar.

Each day has an objective, an action, an output, and a way to check you actually did it.

---

# Week 1 — Target

*Decide who you're going after, and build the first list. No emails this week.*

### Day 1 — Decide what you can service
**Objective:** Know your own limits before you go looking.
**Action:** Write down your radius in miles, the building types you can service, the smallest
contract worth a walkthrough, and anything you can't do (medical waste, high-rise glass, union
sites).
**Output:** Six lines in a notebook.
**Check:** Could someone else read it and correctly reject an account on your behalf?

### Day 2 — Pick two segments
**Objective:** Narrow, so your outreach can be specific.
**Action:** Read **05 ICP and Personas**. Choose two segments. Property management plus one other
is a good default — one relationship there can expose several buildings.
**Output:** Two segments written down.
**Check:** Can you name the job title that signs in each one, without looking?

### Day 3 — Build your first twenty
**Objective:** Get real accounts into the tracker.
**Action:** Follow **04 Prospecting Guide**. Search your segments in your area. Add twenty accounts
to the Account Tracker. Delete the example rows.
**Output:** Twenty rows.
**Check:** Every row has a company, a website or phone, a city, and a segment.

### Day 4 — Qualify them
**Objective:** Cut the list down honestly.
**Action:** Apply the seven filters in **04**. Delete or mark as disqualified anything that fails.
**Output:** Probably eight to twelve accounts left.
**Check:** You deleted at least a third. If you kept all twenty, you weren't strict enough.

### Day 5 — Score what's left
**Objective:** Know what to work first.
**Action:** Score each surviving account. Fit, Value, Access and Service now; leave Trigger at 0.
**Output:** Scored accounts with bands.
**Check:** You can name your top three without scrolling.

---

# Week 2 — Build

*Find the people and the reasons. Still no emails.*

### Day 6 — Learn the triggers
**Objective:** Know what you're looking for.
**Action:** Read **03 Trigger Library** properly. Note which of the eleven you can realistically
check each week.
**Output:** Three or four triggers you'll actually use.
**Check:** For each one, can you name where you'd go to find it?

### Day 7 — Hunt triggers for your top ten
**Objective:** Find real, dated reasons to make contact.
**Action:** For each top account, check the sources in **03**. Log anything you find in the Trigger
Tracker with the exact observation, the source, and the date it happened.
**Output:** Trigger rows. Probably three to five out of ten — that is normal.
**Check:** Every row has a source and a date. Any that don't will score zero, which is the point.

### Day 8 — Find the decision makers
**Objective:** A name, not a job title.
**Action:** Use the cascade in **05**: their website, their Google review replies, your state's
business registry, then LinkedIn.
**Output:** Contact rows.
**Check:** For each, could you say *why* you believe this person signs?

### Day 9 — Verify the emails
**Objective:** Protect your domain before you send anything.
**Action:** Confirm each address is real. A guessed address is a research lead, not something to
send to. Mark status in the tracker.
**Output:** Verified addresses, guesses marked as guesses.
**Check:** No unverified address is about to be emailed.

### Day 10 — Update the scores, pick the batch
**Objective:** A short, deliberate first send.
**Action:** Add the trigger scores. Re-read the bands. Choose the five to ten accounts you'll
contact first — one segment, one trigger type if you can.
**Output:** A named batch in the Campaign Tracker.
**Check:** Everyone in it scores 8 or above and has a verified contact.

---

# Week 3 — Launch

*Send, and handle what comes back.*

### Day 11 — Set up sending properly
**Objective:** Don't damage your main domain.
**Action:** Decide where you're sending from. Put your real business name and postal address in the
signature. Make sure there's a clear way to reply and opt out.
**Output:** A compliant signature block.
**Check:** Would a stranger know who you are and how to make you stop?

### Day 12 — Write the first email
**Objective:** One email, specific to the trigger.
**Action:** Take the matching sequence from **06**. Replace the brackets with what you actually
observed. Read it aloud.
**Output:** One first-touch email.
**Check:** Does every claim about them trace to your Trigger Tracker row? If not, cut it.

### Day 13 — Send the first batch
**Objective:** Five to ten emails. That's all.
**Action:** Send. Log each one in the Follow-up Tracker with a next-touch date.
**Output:** Sent, logged, dated.
**Check:** Every row has a next touch date.

### Day 14 — Handle replies
**Objective:** Answer fast.
**Action:** Read **07 Reply Handling**. Reply to anything human the same day. Suppress any opt-out
immediately.
**Output:** Replies answered, tracker updated.
**Check:** Nothing positive is sitting unanswered overnight.

### Day 15 — Second batch, second touch
**Objective:** Keep the rhythm.
**Action:** Send follow-up two to anyone who hasn't replied. Add another five to ten new accounts.
**Output:** Two touches out on batch one.
**Check:** No account has had two emails in the same week.

---

# Week 4 — Improve

*Look at what happened and fix one thing.*

### Day 16 — Book a walkthrough
**Objective:** Turn a reply into a site visit.
**Action:** For anyone interested, follow **08**. Get the date, time and address confirmed in
writing before you call it booked.
**Output:** A confirmed walkthrough, or a clear next step.
**Check:** Is it in writing? A verbal maybe is not a booking.

### Day 17 — Run it
**Objective:** Measure properly and become the person they want to hire.
**Action:** Follow the checklist in **08**. Take photos. Ask what would make them call you.
**Output:** Measurements, photos, and their answer written down.
**Check:** Could you price the job from your notes without going back?

### Day 18 — Price and propose
**Objective:** A proposal within 48 hours.
**Action:** Price both ways per **08**, then build the proposal per **09**.
**Output:** A three-tier proposal, sent.
**Check:** Does the "what we understood" section use their words?

### Day 19 — Fill in the scoreboard
**Objective:** See the month honestly.
**Action:** Open **11 Metrics Scoreboard**. Enter the last three weeks.
**Output:** Three columns filled.
**Check:** You counted positive replies strictly — "we're covered, thanks" is not positive.

### Day 20 — Change one thing
**Objective:** Improve the weakest step.
**Action:** Find the lowest ratio. If replies are thin, the accounts or triggers were wrong, not the
volume. If replies came but nothing progressed, the problem is the conversation. Change one thing.
**Output:** One decision, written down.
**Check:** Exactly one thing. Not three.

---

## Days 21–30

Repeat weeks 2 and 3 with a bigger batch. Twenty to thirty accounts, not two hundred.

By day 30 you should have a scored list you trust, a trigger habit, several conversations, and
at least one walkthrough. That's what a working month looks like at this stage.

**What it won't look like:** a signed contract in week one. Commercial cleaning decisions take
longer than that, and a system that promised otherwise would be lying to you.

---

## If you fall behind

The order matters more than the schedule. Accounts, then people, then triggers, then contact. Skip
ahead and you'll email the wrong person at a building you can't service about a reason you invented.

If you only get one thing done each week, make it: build ten accounts, find their triggers, contact
them, follow up. That's the whole system, slowly.
