## What this is

A method for finding commercial accounts worth pursuing, identifying the person who signs, spotting
a real reason to contact them now, and running that contact through to a walkthrough.

It is not a list of leads. There are no contacts in this package. You build your own list, for your
own city, using sources that are public and free.

That is deliberate. A list goes stale in ninety days and only works once. A method works every
quarter, in every city you expand into.

---

## The order everything runs in

```
ACCOUNT → PERSON → TRIGGER → OUTREACH → CONVERSATION → WALKTHROUGH → PROPOSAL → WIN or LOSS → LEARN
```

Most people run this backwards. They start with a message, then look for someone to send it to.

Start with the **account** — which buildings you can actually service, at a size worth your time.
Then the **person**, because in a dental office the practice manager signs and the dentist doesn't.
Then the **trigger** — the dated, public reason this account is worth contacting this quarter.

Only then write anything.

## Do this first

**Today, about an hour:**

1. Open **02 Account & Trigger Tracker**. Look at the Quick Start tab.
2. Read **03 Trigger Library** once. Eleven signals. You'll come back to it.
3. Add **ten** accounts from your own city to the Account Tracker. Ten, not a hundred.
4. Score them. The dropdowns do the arithmetic.

**This week:**

5. For the accounts scoring 16 or above, find the person who signs. **05** tells you which role.
6. Find the trigger. Log it with a date and where you found it.
7. Pick the matching sequence from **06**. Send it.
8. Log the send in the Follow-up Tracker.

That's the loop. Everything else in the package makes that loop better.

## If you only remember three things

**A list is not a pipeline.** Names are free. What's scarce is a good reason to call someone this
week.

**A trigger is an event, not a conclusion.** You saw a permit filed. You did not see their cleaning
contract. Say what you observed. Never say what you think it means — that they have no cleaner,
that they're unhappy, that they're shopping. You can't know it, and buyers can tell when you're
guessing.

**Every account you're working needs a next action and a date.** No next action means it isn't in
your pipeline. It's a name on a list.

---

## What this will not do

It won't get you contracts without calls and walkthroughs. It removes the guesswork about who to
contact and when — not the work.

It won't tell you a prospect is unhappy with their cleaner. Nothing can, from the outside.

It won't work if you send four hundred emails in week one. Start with ten accounts you'd be glad to
win.

---

## A note on sending email

Everything here assumes US commercial email. Three things are not optional: your real business
name and a real postal address in every message, a working way for someone to opt out, and honouring
that opt-out immediately and permanently.

This is guidance from one operator to another, not legal advice. If you're unsure, ask someone
qualified — particularly before you start making cold calls, which is a different area of law with
its own rules.
