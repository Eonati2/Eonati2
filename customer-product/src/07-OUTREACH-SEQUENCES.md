# Outreach Sequences

Six sequences, matched to segment and trigger. Adapt the words. Keep the structure.

---

## The four beats

Every first email does the same four things, in this order:

```
1. What you observed        (dated, factual, checkable)
2. What you do              (one line)
3. A conditional offer      ("if X is still being arranged…")
4. An easy question         (a yes/no they can answer in four words)
```

**Beat 3 is where most cold email fails.** *"You need a cleaner"* is a claim about them. *"If
cleaning for the new space is still being arranged"* is a condition — it costs them nothing to
say no to, and it doesn't pretend you know something you don't.

## Rules

- **Under 100 words.** They're reading on a phone.
- **Plain text.** No logo block, no images, no tracking pixel.
- **Three emails, then stop.** A fourth won't win it and costs you the next opening.
- **Never claim** they have no cleaner, are unhappy, or are shopping.
- **Never mention a review** you found.
- **Every email** carries your business name, a real postal address, and a way to opt out.
- **Honour opt-outs immediately and permanently**, email and phone.
- Replace everything in [brackets]. Delete anything that isn't true for you.

# 1. Property management — new building in their portfolio

### Email 1 — day 0 · subject: `the [street] building`
```
Hi [First name] — I noticed [company] recently added the [street] property to
your portfolio.

We handle recurring janitorial for commercial buildings around [area].

If you're reviewing cleaning coverage there, or inherited an arrangement
you're still weighing up, I'd be glad to walk it and give you a scoped number.

Would a short call this week be useful?

— [Your name], [Your company]
```

### Email 2 — day 4 · subject: `re: the [street] building`
```
[First name] — following up briefly.

Two things I'd want to know if I were you: how fast the vendor picks up the
phone, and whether tenant complaints ever reach the owner.

Happy to walk the building and put a scoped proposal together, whether or not
anything changes today.

Worth fifteen minutes?
```

### Email 3 — day 11 · subject: `closing the loop`
```
[First name] — I'll leave it there.

If it's useful, tell me roughly when the current arrangement comes up for
review and I'll check back then rather than filling your inbox.

Either way, good luck with the new property.
```

**Why email 3 works:** it's genuinely final, and it asks for one small fact instead of a decision.
It is reliably the highest-response message in the sequence.

# 2. Medical or dental — new location or move

### Email 1 — day 0 · subject: `the new [street] office`
```
Hi [First name] — I saw [practice] opened at [street] last month.
Congratulations.

We do recurring cleaning for medical and dental offices around [area] —
after-hours, with documented restroom and treatment-room protocols.

If cleaning for the new space is still being arranged, I'd be glad to walk it
and put a scope and price together.

Would Tuesday or Thursday suit for fifteen minutes?
```

### Email 2 — day 4 · subject: `re: the new [street] office`
```
[First name] — one thing worth asking whoever you use: whether they document
what was cleaned, room by room.

For a medical space it's useful to have that on file if anyone ever asks.

Happy to show you what our reporting looks like, no obligation.
```

### Email 3 — day 11 · the close-out.

**Write to the practice manager, not the dentist.**

# 3. Office — move or new location

### Email 1 — day 0 · subject: `your move to [street]`
```
Hi [First name] — noticed [company] moved into [street] recently.

We handle nightly and weekly cleaning for offices around [area].

A move means sorting out a lot of vendors at once. If cleaning is still on
that list, I can walk the space and have a scoped proposal to you in two days.

Would a quick call this week help?
```

### Email 2 — day 4 · subject: `re: your move to [street]`
```
[First name] — briefly.

If you're comparing providers, two questions worth asking each one: will it be
the same crew every time, and what response time will they put in writing.

Those are usually where the differences show up.

Happy to walk the space either way.
```

### Email 3 — day 11 — the close-out.

# 4. Office — facilities hire

### Email 1 — day 0 · subject: `saw the facilities role`
```
Hi [First name] — saw [company] is hiring a facilities manager.

We provide recurring commercial cleaning around [area]. When facilities
becomes someone's actual job, vendor arrangements usually get reviewed at some
point.

No rush on my side. If it'd be useful to have a scoped quote on file for when
that happens, I'm glad to walk the space.

Worth a short call?
```

### Email 2 — day 11 — the close-out.

**Two emails, not three.** This trigger is real but slow. Copy that pretends it's urgent will read
as false, and *"no rush on my side"* is the line that makes it credible.

# 5. Industrial — new facility or renovation

### Email 1 — day 0 · subject: `the [street] facility`
```
Hi [First name] — I saw the [street] facility recently cleared occupancy.

We handle recurring cleaning for industrial and warehouse space around [area]
— floors, restrooms, break areas, and post-construction cleanup where it's
needed.

If cleaning for the new space isn't settled, I'd be glad to walk it.

Would next week work?
```

### Email 2 — day 11 — the close-out.

**Post-construction is a separate job.** Usually larger, usually one-off. Price it separately and
say so on site.

# 6. Schools and childcare

### Email 1 — day 0 · subject: `cleaning coverage for [school]`
```
Hi [First name] — I work with schools and childcare centres around [area] on
recurring cleaning.

I noticed [what you observed].

Two things I'd expect matter here: the same crew each time, and staff who've
been background-checked. Both are things I can put in writing.

Happy to walk the site and put a scope together.
```

### Email 2 — day 11 — the close-out.

**Timing matters more than the trigger.** Before term starts, and before the summer break.

## The close-out, for any sequence

```
[First name] — I'll stop here.

If the timing isn't right, no problem at all. If it changes, the offer to walk
the space stands.

Either way, good luck with [the new space / the year].
```

## Your footer

```
—
[Your name], [Your company]
[Street address], [City], [State] [ZIP]

You're receiving this because [company] appears as a commercial business in
[area]. Reply "no thanks" and I'll remove you right away.
```

Plain and human. It identifies you, gives a real address, and offers a way out — and it reads
better than an unsubscribe-link block.

---

## Subject lines

**Use:** lowercase, short, specific. `the Elm Street building` · `your move to Commerce` ·
`saw the facilities role` · `closing the loop`

**Never:** "Quick question" · "Following up" · anything with an exclamation mark or an emoji ·
"Increase your revenue" · a fake `Re:` on a first email.

**And never a subject that misrepresents the message.** Beyond being bad practice, it's the one
area where email law has real teeth.

---

## Testing

Change one thing at a time. Give it at least a couple of weeks and a few dozen sends before you
conclude anything.

Worth testing, in order: the subject line · whether email 1 names a specific example · how long you
wait before email 2 · whether the close-out asks for a renewal date.

**Not worth testing:** sending more emails to the same people.
