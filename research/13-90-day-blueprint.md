# 13 — The 90-Day Blueprint
**One product · $149 launch · free lead magnet · outbound + organic**

Supersedes the execution plan in `07`. Phases, not a countdown — a phase ends when its gate
passes, not when the days run out.

**The goal is not $10,000.** It is 10 customers, then 25, then 50. Each tells you something the
revenue doesn't (see `12` §The honest consequence).

---

## Days 1–14 · Finish the product

**Objective:** someone can buy it and use it without talking to us.

- [x] 14 customer assets built and audited — `customer-product/`
- [ ] Open both workbooks, confirm no formula errors *(can't be verified in this environment)*
- [ ] Decide the refund policy
- [ ] Package as a ZIP, upload, **test-purchase and confirm the download**
- [ ] Resolve the price: $149, or hold $197 if there are already sales *(`offers/core-os.md`)*

**No automation this phase.** No outbound either.

**Gate:** you have bought your own product and received it.

---

## Days 15–21 · The free product

**Objective:** something genuinely useful to give away.

Build the **Commercial Cleaning Prospecting Starter Kit** — spec in `offers/free-product.md`.
25 triggers, a one-tab tracker, three templates, a four-email nurture. About a day, mostly reuse.

**The test:** someone who downloads it and never buys should still be glad they did. If it reads
as a teaser, rebuild it.

**Gate:** you would send it to someone you respect without apologising for it.

---

## Days 22–30 · First outbound experiment

**Objective:** do they care? Not revenue.

**100–200 highly targeted cleaning companies.** Not more. Small enough that you read every reply.

Measure: replies · positive replies · pack requests · product-page visits · purchases ·
**and the objections, verbatim**.

**The objections are the most valuable output of this phase.** At this volume the rates are noise —
expect two to four positive replies and don't read a percentage into them. What people *say* when
they decline is signal from the first conversation.

**Gate:** you can name the three objections that recur.

---

## Days 31–45 · Fix what the experiment showed

**Objective:** change what the evidence points at, not everything.

Candidates, in the order they usually matter: the offer and the title · the first email · the
landing page · the free product · onboarding after purchase.

**Change one thing at a time.** Three changes at once and you learn nothing.

Then increase outreach — 300–500 contacts, same discipline.

**Gate:** one change made deliberately, with the reason written down.

---

## Days 46–60 · Automate what's repetitive

**Objective:** remove the admin, keep the judgment.

Build in the order in `automation/stack.md`: discovery into Attio, then trigger watching. Those two
are most of the value.

**Do not automate:** who to contact · approving a send · replying to a person.

**Gate:** the weekly trigger check runs without you, and you still approve every batch by hand.

---

## Days 61–90 · Expand

**Objective:** more metros, same method.

DFW was the demonstration. Add metros to *your own* prospect list.

**The customer never buys a DFW list.** The product teaches them to build their own local universe
— that is what makes it sellable nationally.

**Gate:** a second metro producing at the same rate as the first.

---

## What to track

| Phase | The number that matters |
|---|---|
| 1–14 | Does the download work? |
| 15–21 | Would you send it to someone you respect? |
| 22–30 | Recurring objections |
| 31–45 | Did one change move one number? |
| 46–60 | Hours saved per week |
| 61–90 | Second metro vs first |

Revenue is a lagging indicator of all of them. Watch **first 10 → 25 → 50 customers** instead.

---

## What would make me stop and rethink

- **Zero positive replies from 200 targeted contacts.** The offer or the audience is wrong, and
  more volume won't fix either.
- **Pack requests but no purchases.** The free thing is wanted, the paid thing isn't. Either the
  gap between them is wrong, or the price is.
- **Purchases with no usage and refund requests.** The product isn't usable, whatever the audit says.
- **Repeated "can you just do it for me?"** That is evidence for a service — and a decision to make
  deliberately with a decision record, not by improvising on a call.
