### The competitive threat you must plan around
There is already a cluster of agencies publishing **free, detailed, 2026-dated commercial cleaning
cold-email playbooks** — templates, sequences, subject lines, deliverability tips — as lead magnets
for their retainers. The information layer of your product is *already free on the open web.*

**Therefore the product cannot be information.** It has to be the things free blog posts structurally
cannot give: **the built prospect list, the trigger detection, the working automation, and the
one-to-one help.** Every hour you spend writing explanatory prose is an hour spent on the commodity
half of the product. See `02-offer-and-product.md`.

## G. Legal (US-only scope keeps this simple)

| Rule | Detail | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Consent required for B2B cold email in US? | **No.** CAN-SPAM requires no prior consent — the most permissive regime in the world | ⬤⬤⬤ |
| Seven core duties | Honest headers · honest subject · ad disclosure · valid physical postal address · working opt-out · opt-out honored within 10 business days · you're liable for anyone you hire | ⬤⬤⬤ |
| Penalty | **Up to $53,088 per email** (FTC 2026 inflation adjustment); each message is a separate violation | ⬤⬤⬤ |
| State preemption | CAN-SPAM preempts state email statutes (15 U.S.C. §7707) — **except** laws targeting falsity/deception | ⬤⬤ |
| California | B&P Code §17529 gives a **private right of action, $1,000 per email** | ⬤⬤ |
| GDPR | Not engaged if you stay strictly US-targeted | ⬤⬤⬤ |

**Two operational consequences:** (1) a real registered postal address on every send — get a
$10–15/mo CMRA/PO box if you don't want your home address public; (2) **never a false or misleading
"From" name or subject line** — that's the one thing states can still sue over, and California
attaches $1,000 per email to it.

## I. What I could NOT verify — read this before quoting anything back to me

My egress here is restricted to search; I could not open Reddit, Hacker News threads, Gumroad
listings, or the agency blogs directly. That has consequences you should know:

1. **The Reddit and HN anecdotes in the original brief are unverified.** The "appointment in the
   first week from Google Maps outbound," the "hundreds of thousands of emails," the "September 2026
   thread about timing" — I could not open any of them. Treat them as *narrative*, not evidence.
   Nothing in this plan depends on them being true.
2. **Reply-rate and conversion benchmarks are largely vendor-published.** Instantly, Smartlead,
   Saleshandy et al. sell cold email software; their benchmark reports are marketing. I have
   deliberately used the **pessimistic end** of every range in the funnel math for this reason.
3. **The 15–25% signal-based reply figure is the load-bearing assumption of the whole strategy and
   it is ⬤⬤ at best.** `03-funnel-math.md` models it at 12%, not 20%. Week 3 tests it directly. If
   it comes back under 6%, the plan changes — see the kill criteria.
4. **No Gumroad competitor audit.** I could not browse Gumroad. Before launch, search Gumroad
   manually for "cleaning," "janitorial," "commercial cleaning" and check the sales counts on
   anything close. 20 minutes of manual work, and it's the last real unknown.
