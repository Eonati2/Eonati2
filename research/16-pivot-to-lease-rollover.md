# 16 — Pivot: Commercial Cleaning → Commercial Real Estate
**Date:** 2026-09-12 · Status: **decided.** Cleaning product archived, not deleted.

New product thesis: a spreadsheet system that turns a commercial rent roll into a lease-expiration
calendar and a dated action list, sold one-time to commercial property managers and asset managers.

**The pivot is justified and I would make it too.** The buyer has budget, the problem is
operational and recurring, the artifact is spreadsheet-native, and — the part the case for it
didn't make — the audience is *nameable* in a way cleaning owner-operators never were. That last
point is the strongest argument here and it is about distribution, which is the thing that was
actually broken.

Six corrections follow. One changes the product's name.

---

## A. Correction 1 — the product name is in our language, not the buyer's

Checked the US keyword market before committing to "Lease Rollover".

| Term | Volume/mo | CPC | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lease expiration tracker` | **0** | — | 12 |
| `commercial lease tracker` | **no data** | — | — |
| `lease expiration schedule` | 10 | $0 | 15 |
| `lease rollover` | 50 | $0 | 18 |
| `lease rollover risk` | 10 | $0 | 13 |
| **`rent roll template`** | **880** | **$6.46** | 15 |
| `excel rent roll template` | 320 | $5.65 | 15 |
| **`lease abstract template`** | **140** | **$14.35** | 15 |
| `lease administration software` | 260 | **$532.81** | 27 |

**"Rent roll" is 17× bigger than "lease rollover." "Lease expiration tracker" is zero.**

This is not an SEO argument — the plan correctly does not depend on SEO. It is evidence about
**the words the buyer uses for this artifact.** A cold-email subject line and a Gumroad title have
about two seconds to be recognised. "Rent roll" is the document already on the property manager's
desk every month. "Lease rollover" is analyst language for a concept derived from it.

**Recommended name change:**

> ~~Commercial Lease Rollover & Renewal Control Center~~
> **The Commercial Rent Roll Control Center**
> *Turn your rent roll into a lease expiration calendar and a 90-day action list.*

Lead with the noun they recognise; let the subtitle carry the rollover analysis, which stays the
actual differentiator. Same product, findable name.

**The $532.81 CPC on `lease administration software` is the single best number in this research.**
Enterprise lease-admin vendors pay over five hundred dollars a click for this buyer. That is hard
evidence the pain is expensively monetised — far stronger than any forum thread. It also says we
are adjacent to a category with well-funded incumbents, so the positioning has to be "the
spreadsheet before you need software," not "cheaper software."

## B. Correction 2 — $199 flat. The $149 launch price reopens a hole we closed yesterday.

The proposal is $149 launch → $199 standard. We retired exactly that structure on 2026-09-11, and
the reasoning is product-independent arithmetic:

| Price | Sales for $10,000 gross | Subscribers the list must produce |
|---|---|---|
| $149 | 68 | **~1,770–6,000** |
| $199 | 51 | **~1,200–4,300** |

Lower price → more sales needed → more audience to build. Since no channel currently produces a
single download, halving the number that has to be produced is worth more than a discount is.
**Launch at $199. No launch discount.**

**The honest counter-argument, which the case for the pivot surfaced and then didn't address:**
every comparable with a visible sales count is at the low end — $49 with 813 sales, $59 with 129
sales. There is no evidence of volume at $149+ in that scan. That is a real warning.

Two reasons it does not overturn $199, and one caveat:
1. Those are **residential landlord** products sold to individuals. Ours is B2B to a company.
2. A category whose software tier pays $532/click is not a $49 category.
3. **Caveat:** neither point is measured. If $199 produces no sales at all after a real test,
   the price is the first thing to move — but move it on data, not in advance.

## C. Correction 3 — one forum thread is a signal, not evidence

The case describes a Reddit CRE thread as *"unusually good evidence."* It is one thread, and
`revenue-engine` §6 is explicit: do not manufacture market evidence; label every assumption as an
assumption.

What the thread actually supports: **a hypothesis that some CRE professionals find lease-expiry
visualisation awkward in Excel.** Good hypothesis. Worth building a free tool to test. Not
validation, and it should not be cited later as if it were.

The keyword data in §A is better evidence than the thread, because it is a measurement of many
people's behaviour rather than a few people's stated opinion. Lead with it.

## D. Correction 4 — the 8.7/10 score is 8.6, and it should not exist

`(9+9+10+9+8+9+7+7+9) ÷ 9 = 77 ÷ 9 = 8.56`. Rounds to **8.6**, not 8.7.

The arithmetic slip is minor. The number is the problem: nine unmeasured judgements, each scored
to a whole number on an undefined scale, averaged to one decimal place. It reads as a measurement
and is a feeling with a decimal point attached. Our own evidence standard forbids exactly this.

Keep the nine factors — the *thinking* is good and the factor list is the right list. Drop the
score. "Strong on pain, delivery and targeting; weakest on direct competition and SEO" says
everything the 8.6 says, without pretending to precision nobody has.

## E. Correction 5 — a better product does not fix a distribution problem

The case says *"the $10k math becomes reasonable."* It does not become reasonable because the
product improved. The arithmetic is unchanged:

Outbound capacity is still ~3,000 contacts in 90 days — that is Instantly Growth's upload cap, and
it does not care what we sell. At $199, 51 sales, outbound carries 8–15 of them. **The list still
has to produce roughly 1,200–4,300 qualified downloads**, 13–48 a day for ninety days.

Changing the product changes who we talk to. It does not change how many people have to hear about
it. That remains the unsolved problem, and it is the same size today as it was yesterday.

## F. What genuinely does improve — and it is the real case for the pivot

Distribution gets materially better, for a reason the proposal didn't name:

**This audience has named, countable, organised gathering places. The cleaning audience did not.**

| | Cleaning | CRE |
|---|---|---|
| Trade association | BSCAI — best match found, size unverified | **BOMA International — 30,000+ members, local chapters in most major metros** |
| Second association | ISSA — supplier-oriented, wrong half of the industry | **IREM** — property managers specifically |
| Communities | 8 Facebook groups, **membership sizes unverifiable** | Named associations with published membership and chapter events |
| Findable on Apollo | Owner-operators, inconsistent titles | **Property Manager · Asset Manager · Portfolio Manager · Director of Property Management** — standard corporate titles |

Channel C (communities) and D (trade media) were the two unproven hypotheses carrying the
distribution plan. Both get substantially more credible here. **That is the strongest argument for
this pivot and it is worth more than the product comparison.**

Association membership figures are organisation-published; cite them as such and verify before
planning against them.

## G. Correction 6 — build the free version first, not second

The proposal is: build the product, then test the free version. **Invert it.**

The unknown is not whether we can build a good spreadsheet. It is whether property managers want
one enough to trade an email address for it. Build the **Lite** version first — 10 lease rows, one
expiry chart, one action list — and use it as the demand test. Only build the eight-tab paid
version once the free one is being downloaded by real property managers.

This costs days instead of weeks, attacks the actual unknown, and means a failed thesis costs a
weekend. It is also what `revenue-engine` §8 already requires: 100 prospects as a listening
exercise before anything scales.

**This is the third product direction in this project.** That is not a criticism — each change has
been better-reasoned than the last. But it is the reason to make this one cheap to be wrong about.

## H. Correction 7 — eight tabs fights the promise

The stated advantage over the cleaning product is *"they won't feel like they don't know how to
set it up."* Eight tabs works against that.

Tabs 3 (Rollover Calendar), 5 (Renewal Action Board), 7 (Renewal Pipeline) and 8 (Executive
Dashboard) are four time-based views of one dataset. The identified killer feature — *"what do I
need to act on now"* — argues for fewer surfaces, not more.

**Proposed six:**

| # | Tab | Why it survives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Start Here** | 5-minute setup. Non-negotiable for a no-setup promise. |
| 2 | **Lease Register** | The one input sheet. Everything else is derived. |
| 3 | **Action Now** | RED/YELLOW/GREEN by notice deadline. **Opens by default.** This is the product. |
| 4 | **Expiry Calendar** | Year-by-year SF and rent rolling. The analysis that justifies the price. |
| 5 | **Exposure & Risk** | Concentration by year and by tenant. Merges old 4 and 6. |
| 6 | **Renewal Pipeline** | Not Started → Research → Contact → Negotiating → Renewed / Backfill. |

Old Tab 5's 180/120/90/60/30-day buckets become *columns* inside Tab 3, not a seventh sheet.

**One product-design addition, and it may be the real headline.** The field list already contains
`Notice deadline`. In commercial leasing that is the expensive, irreversible date — an option
window that closes takes the renewal right with it, or triggers an auto-renewal on terms nobody
chose. Rollover concentration is analysis; a missed notice date is money already lost. Sort Tab 3
by notice deadline, not by expiry.

Stated carefully, and with the disclaimer in §I: we organise dates the user enters. We do not
interpret leases.

## I. Confirmed correct, no change

**Not marketing this as a legal or compliance tool.** Correct, and important. The product organises
dates and figures the user enters; it does not interpret lease language. A plain disclaimer —
confirm all terms against the executed lease and qualified counsel — ships on Tab 1 and on the
sales page. `revenue-engine` §4 already forbids claiming legal compliance without verification.

**Not building the CRM automation until the product exists and the free version has been tested.**
Correct, and it matches the existing build order.

**Excel + Google Sheets + short PDF, one-time, no subscription, no setup service.** Correct. It is
the same single-product discipline that removed the service tiers on 2026-09-11, applied to a
product where it fits better.

**The cold-email opening — "how are you currently keeping an eye on which leases are rolling in
the next 6–12 months?"** Good instinct: a question about their current process rather than a
pitch. It needs the vocabulary fix from §A before it ships, and it goes through the copy chain.

**Apollo + Apify → Make → Instantly → free tool → nurture → Gumroad.** Sound. Apify is a new line
item; it needs a cost and the same data gate as everything else — public data only, nothing behind
authentication, source and date recorded on every field.

## J. What transfers, and what dies

**The pivot replaces the product and the audience. It does not replace the machine.**

| Survives unchanged | Why |
|---|---|
| Instantly Growth + 8 mailboxes + ramp + domain rule | Sending infrastructure is audience-independent |
| The eight Make jobs | Same shape, different payload |
| Attio, the approval gate, suppression, compliance | Same obligations |
| `building-distribution` and its arithmetic | Capacity is 3,000 contacts regardless of product |
| The evidence standard and safety gates | The reason this document exists |
| CRM Instance A, 10 stages | Product-agnostic funnel; one rename |

| Dies | Replaced by |
|---|---|
| 14 cleaning product assets | The Rent Roll Control Center |
| Free Prospecting Starter Kit | Rent Roll Lite |
| Cleaning ICP, personas, triggers | CRE equivalents |
| Instance B (13-stage contract pipeline) | Nothing — CRE buyers have their own systems |
| `commercial-cleaning-sales`, `cleaning-os-voice` skills | Retired from routing |
| Gumroad sales page | Rewritten |

Roughly the infrastructure half of the repo survives. **That is what makes this pivot cheap, and
it is an argument for making it now rather than after more had been built on top.**
