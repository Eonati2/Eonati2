email only) → `cleaning-os-voice` final → evidence validation against `Trigger_Event.evidence` →

**Nothing sent. CRM untouched. No automation started.**

**Verdict:** the sequences were written to the right rules and still broke them in five places. Two
were serious — an unsupported claim about competitors, and fabricated social proof. Both survived
the original drafting precisely because they *sound* like reasonable sales copy. That is the case
for running the chain rather than trusting the author's intent.

**Change discipline:** 11 edits across ~470 lines. The strategic purpose, structure and CTA of
every sequence is unchanged.

### Why 2 and 3 are the most dangerous

*"Most managers I work with want two things"* is not a style problem. **If there are no managers
yet, it is a lie** — and it is the kind that gets found out, because the first thing a property
manager does when interested is ask who else you work with. It also ships inside the product, so it
would put the same sentence in every customer's outbound.

The rewrite keeps the useful content and drops the false frame: *"Two things I'd want to know if I
were you…"* Same insight, offered as judgment rather than borrowed credibility, and true on day one.

### Why 1 matters beyond honesty

*"Most janitorial companies don't do it"* is a claim about competitors the sender cannot evidence,
in a small local market where those competitors are known to the buyer. It is also unnecessary —
the value of room-by-room documentation stands without disparaging anyone.

## What did not change, and why

**The CTAs.** `cold-email-copywriting` defaults first-touch to a Conditional Question
(*"If I could show you X, would that be worth a look?"*). Our asks are *"Want the list?"* (Instance
A) and *"I'd be glad to walk it"* (Instance B).

**Both are stronger here than a Conditional Question**, for the same reason: they are offers, not
value claims. A Conditional Question still asks the reader to accept a hypothetical benefit;
*"want the list?"* asks them to accept a thing that already exists and costs them nothing. Worth
testing as a variant, not worth adopting as the default.

**Message length.** All emails are 50–125 words, within the skill's range.

**Tone.** No hype, urgency, or scarcity was present to remove. The give-first framing was already
doing that work.

## What this pass demonstrated

The chain caught defects the author's own rules did not prevent — five unsupported claims written
by someone who had just finished writing the rule against them. That is the argument for the voice
layer being a **gate** rather than a guideline.

It also showed where the evidence rule does real work. The two worst findings were both invisible
to a style check: *"most managers I work with"* contains no banned word, no hype, no AI tell. It
fails only against *"can you evidence this?"* — which is why the chain puts evidence validation
after de-slopping, not before.
